Ways to Work With Us

Planning comes first. Then you decide.

Every Pointer Creek relationship begins with planning — understanding what your money is intended to accomplish before recommending any strategy. From there, you choose what responsibilities to keep and what to delegate. Our services are priced separately, so you never pay for what you don’t need.

“What should we do?”

Start with a Fiduciary Planning Project — we analyze the situation, build the architecture, and provide the roadmap.

“How does the planning continue?”

Choose Ongoing Fiduciary Planning — we proactively monitor, coordinate, and advance the plan.

“Who will manage the portfolio?”

Choose Ongoing Fiduciary Wealth Management — we assume ongoing fiduciary responsibility for the assets under our management.

Every relationship begins with planning, but that doesn’t mean every client needs a comprehensive plan. A planning project can be narrowly focused on a single decision — a rollover analysis, Social Security timing, a Roth conversion, a premarital package, estate settlement — or encompass a family’s entire financial life. It has a defined beginning, scope, and conclusion: we analyze the agreed-upon matters, develop recommendations, and provide an implementation roadmap. When it concludes, responsibility for implementation and monitoring generally returns to you.

Consider a project when you have a specific question to address, you’re comfortable implementing recommendations yourself, or you want an independent fiduciary analysis without necessarily establishing an ongoing relationship. Clients who disengage are always welcome to return.

Once you have an architecture, the next question is: who’s responsible for keeping it current? With a project, that returns to you. With Ongoing Fiduciary Planning, you delegate the bulk of it to us. Rather than waiting for you to recognize the next need, we proactively review planning issues, identify triggers, advance priorities, coordinate with your CPA and attorney, and revisit strategies as circumstances change — on a defined planning calendar with scheduled milestones throughout the year.

Consider it when you want someone other than yourself responsible for remembering what happens next, your tax/estate/retirement decisions frequently interact, or your circumstances change often enough that a five-year check-in isn’t enough. It’s generally priced with an initial design-and-architecture fee plus a recurring advisory retainer — based on complexity and responsibility, not net worth alone.

Planning establishes what an investment account is meant to accomplish; this puts that strategy into practice. When we accept assets for ongoing management, we assume fiduciary responsibility for portfolio construction and allocation, investment selection, monitoring, trading and quarterly rebalancing, risk controls, cash and liquidity, distributions, and tax-aware management where applicable.

This is for the portfolio. It does not, by itself, mean we’ve accepted responsibility for continuously monitoring every aspect of your financial, tax, estate, or business planning — that belongs within Ongoing Fiduciary Planning. Not every planning client is an appropriate fit for ongoing investment management, and where it isn’t, we can still provide planning and analysis for assets you manage elsewhere.

Most clients ultimately use more than one model, and many delegate both ongoing planning and ongoing investment management to us. None of these choices is inherently better than another. We intentionally price the services separately so you can select the responsibilities you want us to assume without automatically paying for services you may not need.

When we provide both ongoing planning and ongoing wealth management, certain activities become more efficient — statements, transfers, and third-party coordination fold into one relationship. In those cases we apply Integrated Relationship Pricing: a separate planning fee coexisting with a reduced asset-management rate, so the total reflects the actual combination of responsibilities.

The appropriate relationship depends upon what responsibilities you want to retain — and what responsibilities you want to delegate.
