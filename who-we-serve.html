Who We Serve Best

When we meet with a prospective client, we’re very candid: you’re interviewing us just as much as we’re interviewing you. Financial planning is a long-term, relationship-driven process. We spend a great deal of time with our clients, walk with them through major life decisions, and become intertwined with their families and their futures. Because of that, fit matters. A lot.

We’ve encountered situations where a prospect arrived with several million dollars but spoke poorly to our staff or their own spouse. In those cases, we’ve politely declined the relationship. We can afford to do this because Pointer Creek isn’t built to just gather large accounts; it’s built around mutual respect and shared values. Life’s too short to partner with people who make the work harder than it needs to be.

That said, we do tend to work with clients who share a certain financial profile. Most of our clients do have high six-figure to seven-figure portfolios, and we publish a minimum portfolio requirement. But that doesn’t make us unapproachable if you’re not there, yet. We have a long history of serving children of multigenerational client families, and we a soft spot for hardworking people who are genuinely trying to build up to that.

This is exactly why we always lead with planning projects and offer ongoing fiduciary planning services in addition to ongoing fiduciary investment management. One of our core beliefs is that high-quality, fiduciary advice shouldn’t be reserved only for clients that already have millions. Young entrepreneurs, early-career professionals, and disciplined do-it-yourselfers all deserve access to real guidance without being pushed into products or asset minimum requirements they’re not ready for. Many of our strongest long-term relationships began with planning, and that very planning generated the asset balances we now manage.

We also work with a number of contract professionals — executives, consultants, medical specialists, entertainers, athletes, and others whose income arrives in large, sometimes unpredictable waves. What matters to us isn’t the size of the paycheck; it’s whether someone brings a basic level of discipline, responsibility, and partnership to the process.

If someone’s financial life requires daily guardrails — such as monitoring spending every night, approving discretionary purchases, or managing lifestyle decisions down to the hour — that’s not what we do. If you need a firm to tell you not to order bottle service at the club or to put a hold on chartering a G650 to the beach for the weekend, there are advisory groups built for that. Pointer Creek focuses on solving real problems for real families through thoughtful planning, not lifestyle policing.

We also aren’t a fit for people who expect their advisor to chase the “hot trade” of the week. If you want someone to call you with stock tips for bragging rights on the golf course, we are not your firm. Our portfolio philosophy is grounded in academic research, decades of evidence, and a long-term approach to building durable wealth — not performance theater.

But if you’re looking for an advisor who will help ensure your family legacy, dignity, and long-term financial independence become a reality — and who will tell you the truth even when that truth is uncomfortable — then we will likely work very well together.

Our client profile can be boiled down to this:

We work with clients who are responsible stewards of their resources, value honest guidance, choose thoughtful planning over noise, and want a long-term partner who sees the unseen alongside them.

Life Situations We Work Best With

While we serve a diverse range of entities, trusts, families, and individual clients — including private businesses, family limited partnerships (FLPs), private foundations, non-profits, 501(c) organizations, retirement plans, and endowments — there are several change situations where Pointer Creek consistently delivers exceptional value. These aren’t “client types.” They are inflection points where thoughtful, multidisciplinary planning matters.

Newly Formed Entities & Startup Architecture

Entity choice, tax setup, and retirement structure — set right on day one.

Read more →

We help founders and new business owners make early decisions that will influence their financial and tax lives for decades. This includes:

  • evaluating entity types (LLC, S-corp, C-corp, partnership, etc.),
  • understanding tax classifications and long-term implications,
  • assembling the right professional team (legal, tax, accounting, payroll, benefits),
  • establishing retirement plans that use tax-deferred or tax-free dollars,
  • designing early-stage compensation and ownership structures,
  • implementing tax-managed strategies from day one.

Wise decisions on day one can save extraordinary amounts of money, time, and stress later. We help founders start strong.

We build our systems to excel at guiding entities and families through complex structural transitions, such as:

Business Owners Approaching Transition

Sale, succession, or exit. Like sending a child off to college — we help.

Read more →

One of our greatest areas of strength is working with business owners preparing for valuation, succession, or transition to the next phase of life. For many owners, their business has been intertwined with their identity for decades. Personal and professional finances often sit in one tightly woven knot — emotionally, legally, operationally, and financially.

We help owners unravel that knot with care, clarity, and decades of experience. Whether the transition involves:

  • partial retirement,
  • employee takeover or an internal successor,
  • an outright sale or merger,
  • liquidation,
  • a phased step-back, or
  • a complete and permanent exit,

we provide the financial, tax, legal-structure awareness, emotional grounding, and lifestyle planning needed to make the decision one they will not regret.

Letting go of a business can feel like sending a child off to college. We help owners navigate every part of that process — the logic, the math, and the meaning.

The Retirement Red Zone

“What got you here won’t get you there.” When saving becomes spending.

Read more →

We work extremely well with individuals and couples approaching an impending retirement date — the retirement red zone. This season often drives people to seek a new advisor because, as we tell clients:

“What got you here, won’t get you there.”

We say this because saving for retirement is very different from spending in retirement. A portfolio built for growth or self-directed accumulation may not be structured to deliver protected, tax-efficient, dependable income for decades.

We help clients transition from accumulation to distribution by analyzing:

  • asset allocation and asset location,
  • risk capacity vs. risk tolerance,
  • sequence-of-return risk,
  • Social Security interactions,
  • pension timing and optimization,
  • tax brackets and withdrawal strategies,
  • healthcare and long-term care planning,
  • the emotional and lifestyle adjustments that come with retirement.

We help clients enter retirement not just confident — but fully prepared.

Complex Benefit Systems & “More Than One” Scenarios

Multiple pensions, WEP/GPO, Railroad Retirement — overlapping rules, untangled.

Read more →

We routinely guide clients through the “more-than-one” world. Think of multiple pensions, multiple retirement systems, and overlapping rules that collide in unexpected ways. Examples include:

  • Social Security combined with (STRS) or State Teacher Pension
  • Railroad Retirement paired with other systems
  • Windfall Elimination Provision (WEP) and Government Pension Offset (GPO)
  • Spouses with entirely different benefit structures, vesting schedules, and retirement timelines.

These situations demand precision, context, and clarity — which we provide through vast planning experience.

Premarital Planning, New Couples & Blended Families

Shared values, separate assets, trusts and titling — future friction reduced.

Read more →

We work closely with couples at the beginning of their financial journey together, whether a first marriage, a blended family, or late-in-life partnership. We help them plan to:

  • build shared financial values
  • identify hidden conflicts early
  • align expectations around lifestyle and long-term goals
  • coordinate separate assets responsibly
  • prepare for future family considerations (children or no children)
  • support entrepreneurship, relocation, or career changes
  • manage multi-state property ownership
  • structure trusts, LLCs, and titling to avoid probate

Our process is designed to establish long-term stability and reduce future friction. If you elect ongoing fiduciary planning after your initial plan package, we can support not only the initial level-setting and benchmarking, but ongoing support of keeping the household coordinated across combined goals, combined benefits and combined professionals.

Families with Legacy Property

Cabins, lake houses, hunting land, and ranches — preserved for future generations.

Read more →

We have a particular depth in helping families plan for legacy properties — cabins, lake houses, beach homes, ski chalets, land, or ranches. We can lead and coordinate both planning and implementation for any multigenerational legacy property that holds meaning beyond its market value, along with any necessary supporting endowment funds to sustain that property and prevent unintended disputes and consequences.

This work draws on the principles outlined in our whitepaper Keeping the Cabin in the Family. We help families:

  • articulate legacy intentions
  • create governance frameworks
  • determine ownership structures (trusts or LLCs)
  • establish funding or endowments for maintenance
  • prevent disputes before they occur
  • transition stewardship smoothly to the next generation

A legacy property can be a blessing or a battleground — we help ensure it holds families together as intended.
