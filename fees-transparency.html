Fees & Transparency

Transparency should not be rare.

We publish our fees for a simple reason: fairness and consistency. Here’s how we get paid, why we don’t require assets to work with you, and why our incentives are built to align with yours.

See Published Pricing & Documents

We’re compensated in two primary ways, because different clients — and different situations — require different approaches. For clients who prefer a fully outsourced, do-it-for-me approach, we offer turnkey fiduciary managed accounts: a fully discretionary service where we take responsibility for investment selection, trading, rebalancing, monitoring, and distributions. For clients seeking guidance beyond investments — or before them — we offer structured, flat-fee financial planning project packages designed so you never face open-ended billing anxiety. And for targeted needs, a la carte / hourly planning lets you address a specific question without a full engagement.

Each year, we benchmark planning and wealth-management firms in our region — often with the help of finance interns from local universities. The results are consistent: more than 90% of responses effectively come down to “it depends.”

We made a deliberate decision to do it differently. By publishing our fees, clients understand what to expect before engaging, there’s no advantage to being a more assertive negotiator, no client subsidizes another’s discount, and no one is overcharged simply because pricing wasn’t clear upfront. We know the services we provide and the effort required to deliver them well — and we’re comfortable standing behind that pricing publicly.

Assets alone don’t tell the whole story — and we don’t believe that’s fair. A surgeon completing residency, an entrepreneur building a business, a family expecting a future inheritance, or someone navigating a complex tax or estate decision often has legitimate, complex needs without a large balance yet. Under a traditional AUM-only model, these people are frequently underserved or turned away. Some of our most valuable long-term relationships began before assets accumulated. Our structure lets us serve both — fairly.

We believe all good results come from planning. There’s no responsible way to invest assets without first understanding when they’ll be needed, how they’ll be used, what risks are acceptable, and what outcomes you’re trying to achieve. Without planning, clients often receive cookie-cutter recommendations — the same allocation, the same products — regardless of their situation. Thoughtful planning leads to better decisions, better decisions lead to better outcomes, and better outcomes build long-term trust. Our goal isn’t to gather assets quickly; it’s to understand clients deeply and build recommendations that hold up over time.

Our incentives are intentionally structured to align with our clients — not with short-term production goals. We don’t operate under quarterly asset-gathering targets, production quotas, or directives from outside shareholders. As an independent, owner-led firm, we can make decisions with a long-term perspective — often measured in decades, not quarters — and we have no intention of becoming a publicly traded company. Our approach is more aligned with mutual and cooperative, long-term stewardship models: what is the best decision for the client’s life, family, and long-term outcome — not what looks best on a quarterly report.

Our billing structure exists for one reason: fairness. Across hundreds — ultimately thousands — of advisor interactions before founding Pointer Creek, one thing became clear: most firms struggle to define and consistently apply pricing because of internal inconsistency. We built a structure where pricing is clearly defined, services are consistently delivered, and clients understand exactly what they’re paying for.

Our commitment to fairness goes further. There have been situations where clients engaged for a certain level of service but, due to time or life circumstances, couldn’t fully use it — and we proactively reduced fees, on our own initiative. That’s not a theoretical philosophy; it’s something we’ve done repeatedly. Clients should never feel they’re overpaying, underserved, or misunderstood.
