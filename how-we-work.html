Our Process

How we actually work.

Most advisory firms describe what they sell. Very few explain how they actually work. At Pointer Creek, process matters — not because we love complexity, but because thoughtful planning, accountability, and follow-through are what protect clients from mistakes, missed steps, and conflicts of interest. Here is exactly how our real process works, from the first conversation to long-term partnership.

Start With an Introductory Meeting

Most clients arrive by referral — or after searching specifically for a fiduciary, CFP® practitioner, conflict-free advice.

  • No bought leads, no ads, no billboards or golf outings.
  • We often keep a waiting list rather than chase growth.

What we hear a lot

“I can’t believe you actually publish your fees.” · “I can’t believe you’ll just charge me for the planning time I use, with no asset requirement.” · “I can’t believe you built me a plan and the answer wasn’t ‘roll everything over to us.’”

However you find us, everyone enters the same way — with an introductory meeting.

It’s a mutual interview, not a presentation. We’re learning who you are and what success looks like — six months or six decades out. You’re evaluating us.

By the end, we commit to three things

  • An honest read on mutual fit.
  • An estimate of time, cost, and complexity.
  • A clear next step — even if it’s pointing you elsewhere.

We’re candid about what we don’t do. Want an advisor to day-trade crypto and turn $10,000 into $10 million? Not us. Want a durable plan that protects your family and uses money intentionally? Likely a strong match.

Hire us for planning and we lead with planning — finishing the full process before implementing any wealth- or risk-management solutions.

The CFP Board’s seven steps

  • Discovery · data gathering · analysis & modeling · recommendations · implementation · coordination · ongoing monitoring.

What isn’t standard

  • Nearly 25 years of complex-planning experience.
  • Comfort with everyone from newlyweds to companies valued over $100 million.
  • A “CFO for hire” gear for high-need situations — without dropping quality for anyone else.
“We don’t guess. We model.”

Related: Financial Planning · Estate Planning · Investment Philosophy.

  • We actively work ten financial plans at a time; finish one, and the next moves from the waiting list to active.
  • Every engagement gets real attention — no rushed analysis, no crowding out existing clients.
  • A typical plan runs four to eight weeks, paced mostly by how fast you return your “homework” (statements, benefit details, Social Security estimates).

Our planning engine is fast; the natural pauses are in information-gathering.

We put unusual energy into documentation and follow-through — where many advisory relationships quietly fail.

  • Meeting notes run through secure, encrypted dictation and internal QC.
  • They become structured tasks in our practice-management software.
  • Our operations manager verifies them; the firm reviews them each morning.
  • A $10 million client and a $1 million client get the same checklist-driven care.

We don’t leave your financial life to chance, inbox chaos, or someone’s sticky note.

  • Estate funding & “if you died tomorrow” testing. We go asset by asset — from the knickknacks to the multi-million-dollar account — and run a “paper assassination” exercise: a simulated walk-through of what would actually happen. It has helped clients avoid millions in probate costs.
  • Deep-dive risk management analysis. With no allegiance to any insurer, our models test multiple carriers and scenarios — comparisons that would “wrap around the conference room.” Our loyalty is to the outcome, not the company.
  • Balancing wealth, life, and legacy. We’re not here to tell people to hoard every dollar. It’s not unusual for us to say, “You can afford to spend some of this.” The point isn’t the largest spreadsheet; it’s a life and legacy that reflect your values.

We’re a completely independent, owner-controlled firm — which means:

  • No AUM quotas.
  • No annual growth targets.
  • No production goals tied to products or platforms.

Those introduce conflicts that pull attention away from fiduciary advice. If you’ve seen Field of Dreams, it’s an “if you build it, they will come” mentality.

Our incentives are aligned with getting the planning right — not with gathering as many assets as possible.
